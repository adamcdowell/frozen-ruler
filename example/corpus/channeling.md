# Channeling

Channeling is water finding a path of least resistance through the puck —
part of the bed gets over-extracted while the rest is barely touched. The
symptom: shots that run faster than the grind setting predicts, spritzing from
the bottomless portafilter, and a sour-and-bitter-at-once cup.

Causes, in rough order of frequency: clumpy grounds, uneven distribution,
tamping at an angle, and headspace gaps. The fixes are puck prep, not grind:

- WDT (Weiss Distribution Technique): a fine needle tool to break up clumps
  and level the bed before tamping. The single highest-value prep step.
- A level, consistent tamp — pressure matters far less than being flat.
- A puck screen reduces water-jet disturbance on the top of the bed.

If shots still run fast at a fine setting after clean prep, revisit
[[dialing in espresso]] — the recipe itself may be off.
