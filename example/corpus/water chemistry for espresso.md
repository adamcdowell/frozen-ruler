# Water chemistry for espresso

Water is 90%+ of the cup and the main determinant of machine lifespan. Two
numbers matter most:

- General hardness (GH) — the calcium and magnesium that do the flavor work.
  Too low and extraction tastes flat; too high and you brew chalk.
- Alkalinity (KH) — the buffer. Its role is to stabilize acidity: too little
  and the cup turns sharp and sour; too much and it flattens the acidity that
  makes coffee interesting, while feeding scale.

A common espresso target: GH around 50–80 ppm, KH around 40 ppm. Scale forms
from temporary hardness precipitating in the boiler — which is why remineralized
RO or bottled recipes beat tap in hard-water areas. Descaling schedules and
routine backflushing live in the [[cleaning and maintenance log]].
