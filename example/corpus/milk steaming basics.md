# Milk steaming basics

Good microfoam is paint-like: glossy, no visible bubbles, pours like wet
paint. Two phases: stretching (tip near the surface, introducing air, the
paper-tearing sound) and texturing (tip submerged, whirlpool rolling, breaking
big bubbles into micro ones).

Stretch only in the first few seconds while the milk is cold. Stop adding air
by 35°C, then texture until the pitcher reads 55–60°C for a flat white or
60–65°C for a latte. Past 68°C the sweetness collapses and the proteins
scald — there is no recovering scalded milk.

Whole milk is the most forgiving; oat drinks with a barista formulation
texture closest to dairy of the alternatives.
